Customer Churn: Modelling Report

What model I built, why, how well it works, what it says about churn drivers, and what it means for the business. The EDA choices that led here are documented in the companion EDA report.

Author: Khaled El-Shamouty  ·  2026-05-21

1. The modelling brief (in one sentence)

Rank 50,000 customers by their probability of churning, so a retention team with a finite budget can decide which customers to contact : and where to draw the line.

That framing matters. It makes this a ranking problem, not a classification problem. The right metrics are therefore precision@K and PR-AUC (how good is the top of the ranked list?), not accuracy (how often is the binary label right?).

2. Model choice

The EDA found that Lifetime_Value : the feature with the highest mutual information with churn : has essentially zero linear correlation with it (r = −0.01). The signal is non-linear. A logistic regression model can't use it. A gradient-boosted tree model can, trivially.

Why HistGradientBoostingsklearn's HistGradientBoostingClassifier: handles non-linear feature effects, native missing-value support, scale-invariant, and zero extra dependencies (ships with scikit-learn). Trained with 400 boosting rounds at learning rate 0.05. No grid search : CV variance was too small to justify it.

3. Validation approach

  • Stratified 80/20 split : 40,000 train, 10,000 test set (set aside until final eval).
  • 5-fold stratified CV on the train set to check stability before committing to the test set.
  • All reported numbers below are on the untouched 20% test set.

7. Choosing an operating point

The model outputs a probability for each customer. The business picks a threshold above which a customer gets a retention offer. That choice trades precision against recall : and budget against coverage.

At threshold 0.30: cast wide, capture 85% of true churners, flag ~29% of the base : a high-recall campaign. At 0.70: surgical, 96% precision, but miss 27% of true churners and flag only 22% of the base. The right threshold depends on the relative cost of a missed churner vs a wasted offer : a business decision, not a modelling decision.

8. The business case : savings curve

To translate model performance into business terms, I compute expected net savings at each possible targeting cutoff under three transparent assumptions:

  • $100 retained revenue per churner successfully saved
  • $15 cost per retention offer sent
  • 30% of contacted true churners are actually retained

These numbers are placeholders. The shape of the curve is the finding; the dollar levels only become meaningful when real marketing economics are plugged in.

Expected net savings vs fraction of customers contacted (sorted by predicted churn risk). The curve peaks around 25% : beyond that, the cost of contacting lower-precision customers outweighs the returns.
The actionable findingUnder these toy economics, the optimal campaign targets the top ~25% of customers by predicted risk : contacting 2,500 customers, capturing ~2,306 true churners, and generating estimated net savings of $31,680 per campaign cycle. Beyond that point the campaign spends more than it recovers.

9. Recommendations

For the retention team

  • Target the top 25–30% of customers by predicted churn risk each campaign cycle. That captures ~80% of true churners at >82% precision.
  • Treat service-call volume as a front-line signal. It's the #1 model driver and readable directly from the CRM : no model needed. Customers with 7+ calls in the window are at sharply elevated risk.
  • Flag customers whose behavioural data goes missing, not just those whose metrics decline. A lapsed session or missing email-open record is a leading indicator, not just a gap.

For Product / Analytics leadership

  • Lifetime_Value is not a proxy for churn safety. The model shows a strong non-linear relationship : high-LTV customers churn in ways a simple ranking by value won't reveal.
  • Tenure doesn't predict churn here. If production data agrees, long-tenure customers should not be deprioritised in retention spend.
  • Geography and signup cohort carry no signal. Campaign segmentation by country or quarter is not statistically justified.

Toy economics in savings curve Replace with real offer costs and LTV estimates before making budget decisions.
Lifetime_Value may be contemporaneous If LTV includes post-churn revenue, it's a leakage risk. Audit computation window before deploying.
